Inspired by my college dorm, this room model explores a collection of memories through souvenirs and personal items.


The goal was to create an experience that felt interactive and welcoming, a snapshot into my personal life. A large part of this project was the process of translating the model from a static Blender model to an interactive web experience.

Project Inspiration & Vision

After graduating from college, I found myself searching for images of my full dorm-room in my camera roll but could only find separate snapshots of particular corners or small areas.

In those early days of reminiscing a place I had made my own, I decided to take it to Blender to recreate it virtually, where it could exist as a whole.

3D Look Development

Most of the time I spent in my room was in the evenings, where I had my lamp on and the room took on a warm lighting of coziness.

The items I chose to recreate in this 3D model were made of a variety of different materials, including glass, metal, paper, and wood. It was important to replicate the behaviors of each material using different textures and thinking about how each should react to the room's lighting.

From Blender to Three.js

In order to make this model interactive as a web experience, it had to be translated from its native state in Blender to objects that could be accessed through a live page. The process in Blender involved UV unwrapping the models and baking their textures in order to preserve their geometries, colors, and materials' lighting behaviors.

Afterwards, I began organizing the objects on a webpage using the 3D graphics Javascript library three.js, allowing them to exist in a 3D world outside of Blender.

One of the main challenges of this was programming the camera and mouse controls so that users were able to navigate through the room using their trackpad while staying within designated bounds.

Interaction Delivery

The final step was adding biographies to the main objects in the room. This model allows you to hover over an object and click to read a bit about what makes this object special to me. Some of them have even inspired art that is linked in their bios.

Final Thoughts

The result of this project was an interactive room that was able to exist outside of Blender and allowed users to explore it in a 3D environment powered by three.js. This virtual model successfully models my college dorm in a way that feels complete, warm, and like a representation of my time at Harvard.

I had previously used Blender and three.js each for 3D modeling, but never connected both. I enjoyed learning how to translate the project across the two tools and use them to create something that wouldn't have been possible in one alone.
