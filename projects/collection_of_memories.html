Inspired by my college dorm, this room model explores a collection of memories through souvenirs and personal items.


The goal was to create an experience that felt interactive and welcoming, a snapshot into my personal life. A large part of this project was the process of translating the model from a static Blender model to an interactive web experience.

Project Inspiration & Vision

After graduating from college, I found myself searching for images of my full dorm-room in my camera roll but could only find separate snapshots of particular corners or small areas.

In those early days of reminiscing a place I had made my own, I decided to take it to Blender to recreate it virtually, where it could exist as a whole.

3D Look Development

Most of the time I spent in my room was in the evenings, where I had my lamp on and the room took on a warm lighting of coziness.

The items I chose to recreate in this 3D model were made of a variety of different materials, including glass, metal, paper, and wood. It was important to replicate the behaviors of each material using different textures and thinking about how each should react to the room's lighting.

From Blender to Three.js

What architectural features are preserved after transforming a building’s exterior design via a computational grammar and how does this affect the recognizability of the building?

What elements are truly important to a building’s facade?

Given that Memorial Hall is an example of Gothic architecture, I spent some time analyzing historical architectural features of Gothic buildings to understand the design principles that shape their structures.

Memorial Hall, like many European Gothic buildings, is characterized by its pointed arches, ribbed vaults, lancets, and rose windows. These elements are distributed across the facade using rules of repetition, symmetry, and division of levels.

Creating the Program

The implementation of my project was primarily based on creating a p5.js program that would generate Andrade-style designs based on the extracted features. The program featured 3 levels of abstraction: high, medium, and low.

Evaluation and Next Steps

One of my favorite aspects of this project was analyzing how strategic and computational Andrade's artwork was, as opposed to my first impression that the placing of shapes was simple and straightforward.

This is an important characteristic of rule-based design: it tends to be computational and systematic in order for it to appear as natural to viewer as possible.

The next steps in this project would be to implement a cleaner user interface and additional user interactions.

These additional interactions would allow the user to select the building from which design elements will be generated, as well as setting certain parameters to modify the designs.

Finding the Beauty in Rule-Based Art

Many may assume that rule-based generative art is restricted because it is behaves within a limited set of rules. Yet, artworks such as Edna Andrade's and generative programs such as this one demonstrate that the opposite is true: these systems inspire the creation of an infinite amount of designs made possible due to controlled randomness.

Generative systems and rule-based art have roots that long pre-date computers and even algorithms. You can find them in the geometric tile artworks of 8th-century Islamic architecture, the petals of an intricate rose, and the fractals of a unique snowflake.

Next time you see a repetitive pattern or geometric artwork, think: how would you change it? Even one seemingly small and simple change can open up the door to an infinite number of new, distinct designs.
